# Track 06 — storm / weather development record

**Status:** Draft 01 active  
**Role:** energetic environmental danger / storm-cell traversal  
**Tempo:** 144 BPM  
**Meter:** 4/4  
**Center:** D-minor-centered storm language  
**Current boundary:** Draft 01 — Territory of Weather

## Dramatic function

Track 06 treats a violent storm cell as a territorial environmental encounter.

The central semantic idea is:

> entering the storm means entering the domain of something much larger and more powerful than the player.

This intentionally parallels the territorial logic of Track 02 without turning the cue into a dragon reprise.

The cue should be:

- energetic;
- exciting;
- dangerous;
- kinetic;
- weather-driven rather than combat-driven.

## Cross-cue motif experiment

Track 02's full dragon motif remains:

`E-F-B-Bb / A-G-F-E`

Draft 01 borrows only the opening territorial cell:

`E-F-B`

That cell appears near the beginning as a warning and returns near the end.

The full Track 02 phrase is deliberately withheld.

The storm closes with its own chromatic recoil toward D rather than completing the dragon motif.

Intended meaning:

> you are in something else's territory

not:

> the dragon is here.

## New storm motif

Principal storm motif:

`D-F-A-Eb | C-Bb-G-A`

Canonical rhythm:

- dotted quarter;
- eighth;
- quarter;
- quarter;
- quarter;
- eighth;
- eighth;
- half.

Identity:

- rising D-F-A reach;
- A-to-E-flat tritone fracture;
- falling C-B-flat-G gust;
- final A that prevents total collapse.

Primary owner:

- Violins 1.

This deliberately expands the score away from the repeated clarinet ownership of Tracks 01 and 03.

## Rhythmic engine

Low strings use transformed Skyforge three-versus-two grouping:

- 3+3+2;
- 3+2+3;
- 2+3+3.

Track 02 used 3+3+2 as controlled wingbeat.

Track 06 instead changes group ordering so the same project-level rhythmic family behaves like unstable gusts and crosswind.

## Orchestral roles

- V1: principal storm motif;
- viola/cello/bass: gust motor;
- percussion: pressure and impact, not continuous combat scoring;
- horn: weather-scale / territorial breadth;
- oboe: fragment transfer in active weather;
- high winds: gust texture rather than primary lyrical lead;
- tuned percussion: lightning punctuation;
- bassoon: low territorial warning language.

## Current acceptance questions

1. Does the E-F-B callback read as meaningful lineage rather than a dragon cameo?
2. Is the new storm motif memorable enough to own the cue?
3. Does changing 3+3+2 into 3+2+3 / 2+3+3 feel like buffeting weather?
4. Is the charged-eye section enough relief before the eyewall?
5. Does the track feel exciting rather than merely threatening?
6. Does the closing callback make "territory" feel like a reusable score-wide concept?
