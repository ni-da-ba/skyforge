# MUS-0001 — Skyforge musical language

**Status:** Working language, not a fixed style bible

## Purpose

This document records musical relationships that have survived repeated cue development and listener review.

These are not rules that every cue must obey. The purpose is to identify project-level vocabulary, distinguish it from cue-specific symbolism, and prevent successful devices from becoming automatic habits.

## Project-level vocabulary

### Open harmonic color

Skyforge repeatedly responds well to:

- add9 sonorities;
- major sevenths;
- suspended harmony;
- open fifths;
- inversions that reduce tonic heaviness;
- occasional Lydian / #11 color.

This language supports altitude, openness, curiosity, and strangeness without requiring every cue to sound euphoric.

The same color can change meaning by context:

- Track 00: luminous wonder and revelation;
- Track 01: ordinary-world magical tilt;
- Track 02: unstable or threatening sky territory.

### Three-versus-two rhythmic DNA

The project has repeatedly used grouped motion that contrasts three and two.

Examples:

- Track 00: 6/8 **3+3** against **2+2+2**;
- Track 01: 4/4 eighth-note **3+3+2** walking groove;
- Track 02: **3+3+2** transformed into predatory wingbeat / encounter motor.

The recurring principle is more important than literal meter.

### Horns as scale

Horns have repeatedly worked as a semantic carrier of breadth:

- horizon;
- altitude;
- spatial scale;
- grandeur;
- threatening size.

This is useful but dangerous. Horns should not become the default solution for every scenic event.

### High winds as air

Flute and piccolo can strongly suggest:

- altitude;
- wind;
- glint;
- curiosity;
- aerial motion.

Track 02 exposed the danger of overusing that association. During panic, flute repeatedly pulled attention toward the wrong expressive language and was eventually removed from the entire panic section.

Project rule:

> Do not preserve an instrument because its semantic label seems correct if its perceptual effect is wrong.

## Cue-specific semantic roles

### Track 00 — A Windborne Fantasia

- flute: propulsion / curiosity / upward travel;
- horns: first panorama and later confident re-launch;
- V1: emotional realization at revelation;
- threshold gesture: impending arrival;
- arrival material: surprise / wonder;
- triumph phrase: “we made it,” not military victory.

### Track 01 — Rambling Through the Gentle Blue

- clarinet: traveler perspective in this cue;
- groove: footsteps / forward motion;
- orchestra: the world being noticed;
- horns: distant horizon.

The clarinet role is not automatically a project-wide “player instrument.”

### Track 02 — The Lord of Empty Miles

- low 3+3+2 motor: controlled dragon movement;
- low strings / low brass: physical mass and predatory scale;
- upper agitated strings: human urgency / panic;
- dragon motif: E–F–B–B♭ / A–G–F–E family;
- subtraction after panic: scale becoming comprehensible again.

The core dramatic device is:

> The dragon does not panic. The player does.

## Composition principles retained

1. One foreground owner at a time.
2. Teach an idea before surrounding it with countermelody.
3. Doubling is often more useful than unnecessary counterpoint.
4. Support lines should be horizontally coherent even when perceived as harmony.
5. Large moments grow in scale, not perceptual confusion.
6. Subtraction can make a later sonority feel larger than continued fortissimo.
7. A dramatic function outranks textbook resolution.
8. Barlines do not define phrase endings.
9. Silence is valuable when it punctuates a meaningful sentence; it is not automatically “thoughtful.”
10. A motif must first sound good before its transformability matters.
11. Repetition is useful when the returning material changes meaning.
12. Distinctive chromatic material should not be removed merely for scale purity.
13. Do not solve every adventure or danger cue with percussion.
14. Do not solve every sky cue with flute.
15. Do not polish a frozen cue simply because more editing is possible.

## Track 03 lessons so far

The contemplative cue has been useful precisely because several reasonable approaches failed.

### Failed or weak approach: “quiet Skyforge”

Sparse strings, distant horn, isolated piano/celeste, and open harmony created loneliness but mostly resembled ambient passages already present in Tracks 00 and 01.

Lesson:

> Lower density is not a new emotional identity.

### Failed or weak approach: engineered thoughtfulness

An A-Dorian cue used silence, odd meter, isolated statements, and later a continuous piano figure. The result was conceptually orderly but not memorable enough.

Lesson:

> Thoughtfulness cannot be manufactured from meter, silence, and texture if the underlying melody does not matter.

### Better approach: melodic gate

A short piano/cello sketch finally produced a stronger response. Subsequent work showed that:

- phrase architecture alone is insufficient if melodic notes feel arbitrary;
- a repeated hook improves identity;
- rhythmic complexity applied bar-by-bar can still sound mechanical;
- the next successful step is to write a recognizable multi-bar melody first and let rhythm follow the phrase.

Current Track 03 Sketch 5 therefore uses an A / A′ / B / A″ song-like string form with a recurring cello head:

```text
F -> A -> D -> C
```

The phrase-scale recurrence is the identity. Individual bars are no longer expected to prove their complexity.

## Reference-listening policy

Reference works are used to extract structural lessons, not musical content.

Current analytical references include:

- **Space Junk Road** — Mahito Yokota, *Super Mario Galaxy*;
- **Rosalina in the Observatory** — Koji Kondo, *Super Mario Galaxy*;
- **Outer Wilds** score — Andrew Prahlow.

The relevant lessons are broad:

- calm music can maintain continuous low-pressure support;
- a contemplative cue can still have a strongly recognizable melody;
- repeated themes can gain meaning through orchestration and context;
- accompaniment can sustain motion while the lead phrase breathes.

No third-party audio, MIDI, notation, transcriptions, or melodic data are part of the Skyforge repository.

## Motif recurrence and transformation framework

With Tracks 00-03 frozen, the score now has enough successful authored material to support selective leitmotivic recurrence.

The governing principle is:

> Reuse a motif only when the new dramatic situation has a real semantic relationship to the old one.

Do not insert previous themes merely to make the soundtrack sound interconnected.

### Current motif families

**Track 00 — horizon / revelation family**

Useful source material includes:

- rising woodwind propulsion;
- threshold / impending-arrival gesture;
- open add9 / major-7 / Lydian horizon harmony;
- horn breadth.

Potential future meanings:

- first sight of a new region;
- extraordinary altitude;
- distant legendary geography;
- altered or ruined versions of a once-wondrous place.

**Track 01 — traveler / ordinary-world exploration family**

Useful source material includes:

- clarinet protagonist behavior;
- 3+3+2 walking groove;
- world-response gestures after foreground phrases;
- light harp/celeste discovery punctuation.

Potential future meanings:

- night exploration;
- changed climate or region while remaining in ordinary travel;
- return-home or familiar-place variants;
- tired, hurried, or transformed traveler states.

**Track 02 — dragon / legendary threat family**

Locked source identity:

`E-F-B-Bb / A-G-F-E`

Potential future transformations:

- distant omen: slower, incomplete, thin register;
- territory cue: bass/low-wind fragment without full statement;
- aftermath: ancient chorale / widened intervals;
- related creature or dragon lineage: preserve one contour cell while changing rhythm/timbre.

The full motif should remain reserved for the legendary dragon itself unless fiction later establishes a direct relationship.

**Track 03 — Count the Leagues / reflection family**

Locked source identity:

`D-D-F-Bb | A-E-F`

Potential future transformations:

- homecoming: warmer harmony, less exposed E-F catch;
- old age / long journey: augmentation and lower register;
- solitude in a harsher place: stripped fragment only;
- remembrance: high afterimage or tail fragment without full refrain;
- acceptance / return: canonical head over broadened stable harmony.

### Transformation axes

A previous motif may be varied through:

1. tempo and duration;
2. register;
3. orchestration / timbral ownership;
4. harmonic context;
5. fragmentation;
6. augmentation or compression;
7. rhythmic regrouping;
8. accompaniment identity;
9. cadence or tail behavior;
10. density and surrounding silence.

For recognizability, usually preserve at least two or three of:

- characteristic contour;
- rhythmic fingerprint;
- interval identity;
- timbral association;
- phrase function.

### Narrative rule

A recurrence should answer:

> Why is the player remembering this musical idea now?

If there is no convincing answer, write new material instead.

### Near-term opportunity

A future night-exploration cue is a strong candidate for the first deliberate cross-cue transformation study.

The most promising starting point is **Track 01's traveler language**, not a literal quotation of its clarinet melody:

- slow or soften the 3+3+2 walking identity;
- preserve the idea of the world responding after the traveler;
- move the clarinet into a darker or lower register;
- replace daylight glints with sparse nocturnal color;
- optionally allow a small Track 00 horizon fragment only at a genuinely extraordinary vista.

This would test whether Skyforge can create continuity through transformed behavior and motif grammar without turning the score into self-quotation.
