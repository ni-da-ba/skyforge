## Protected perceptual identity

The motif should remain identifiable through the combination of:

- repeated-D hesitation;
- F-to-B-flat upward reach;
- broad B-flat arrival;
- A-to-E drop;
- exposed E-to-F semitone resolution;
- canonical rhythmic fingerprint;
- clarinet timbre in canonical statements.

The motif is not only a pitch sequence. Rhythm, contour, and timbre materially contribute to recognition.

## Allowed development

The theme may be developed through:

- fragmentation;
- augmentation;
- rhythmic stretching;
- changed harmonic context;
- changed accompaniment;
- phrase splitting between voices;
- limited local timbral transfer;
- altered tail behavior;
- register change;
- subtraction / stripped recollection.

## Development guardrails

Do not simultaneously change pitch contour, rhythm, timbre, and phrase function unless the canonical object has already been re-established nearby.

Do not treat transposition of the entire sentence as the default development method.

Do not add decorative notes merely to make a variant appear more developed.

Do not let a derived version erase the listener's ability to recognize the canonical refrain.

## Governing principle

> The motif is now an object with identity. Development should reveal new meanings of that object rather than continually inventing replacements for it.
