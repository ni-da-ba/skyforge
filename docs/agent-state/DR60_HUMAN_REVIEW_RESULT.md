## Required corrections

### A. Canopy headroom

Jungle trees were tall enough that their tops were visibly chopped off by the upper generation boundary.

**Requirement:** tall vegetation placement must account for remaining vertical headroom. Reject, taper, or substitute vegetation when the expected canopy cannot fit inside the available envelope. Visible canopy truncation is a generation defect rather than acceptable variation.

### B. Hydrology composition

The canonical specimen's hydrology was qualitatively inadequate: approximately one source block spilled directly over the island edge.

**Requirement:** water presence/persistence alone does not satisfy the product gate. Hydrology should form geographically legible features such as basins, ponds, channels, streams, seepage networks, or deliberately composed waterfalls.

### C. Incidental edge discharge

The specimen had extensive water and lava spillage from island sides.

**Requirement:** routine arbitrary fluid discharge should be strongly suppressed. Edge discharge should be uncommon and compositionally or geologically justified. Deliberate waterfalls remain valid; incidental leakage, especially lava, should not be commonplace.

## Gate interpretation

DR-60 is **CHANGES REQUIRED**, not a rejection of the core landform/interior pipeline. The machine gates successfully established deterministic generation, persistence, isolation, and feature presence, while the human review exposed an additional product boundary: mechanical feature presence is not equivalent to successful world composition.

The next dressed-region repair should preserve the coherent surface/cave foundation while correcting vegetation headroom and fluid/hydrology composition, then regenerate the canonical specimen, rerun the required machine evidence, and return to human flight review.
