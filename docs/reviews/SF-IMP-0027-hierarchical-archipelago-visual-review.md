## Scope

This review evaluates the first regional Skyforge hierarchy above the accepted SF-IMP-0026 group layer. The gate is not final world-density aesthetics. It asks whether complete accepted child groups can be composed into a larger regional structure while preserving island separation and producing visually legible higher-level organization.

The required visual questions are:

1. Does a Hub read as one dominant anchor formation plus semantically distinct supporting groups?
2. Does an Arc read as an ordered regional corridor rather than a random scatter?
3. Is deliberate empty sky visible between child formations?
4. Do child-group identities survive realization at regional scale?
5. Does island morphology remain perceptible inside the larger hierarchy?
6. Do the numerical and visual artifacts agree about separation, survival, and clipping?

## Acceptance conclusion

The human visual gate passes.

SF-IMP-0027 demonstrates the first accepted hierarchy in which Skyforge composes independently planned, independently seeded, provider-neutral island groups into a larger regional structure while retaining:

- child-group semantic identity;
- island-member identity;
- visual and numerical group separation;
- deliberate empty-sky corridors;
- morphology diversity;
- vertical variation;
- deterministic regional realization.

The Hub and Arc are meaningfully different structures rather than parameter variations of one scatter algorithm. The Hub establishes central dominance and supporting sectors. The Arc establishes ordered regional flow and corridor spacing.

This is an architectural acceptance, not a claim of final world-composition aesthetics. The main next limitation is density and world-design richness: future regional presets should add controllable sub-hierarchy, denser satellite structures, branching corridors, and eventual biome/material/gameplay semantics without weakening the accepted nested reservation and identity contracts.
