# CIV-0031 — Population and Demand Formation

Status: PRECOMMIT / LOCKED DIRECTION

## Decision

Settlement demand is derived from authoritative civilization settlement state. The Guild/economy layer does not create a second population model and does not simulate household transactions or individual consumer behavior.

Demand is represented as coarse pressures on commodity and good classes, primarily through desired-stock bands, baseline consumption, productive inputs, infrastructure requirements, and temporary event demand.

## Authority boundary

Authoritative inputs may include:

- population / settlement scale;
- prosperity / wealth characteristics;
- productive capabilities and industries;
- settlement role;
- route connectivity and trade intensity;
- infrastructure and fleet activity;
- environment where relevant;
- current shocks, incidents, or projects.

These are consumed by the civilization economic layer. Guild markets, freight, procurement, and routine contracts respond to the resulting demand state rather than independently inventing shortages.

## Demand sources

Demand should be decomposable into a small set of economically meaningful sources:

1. **Population consumption** — food, textiles, ordinary goods, and other broadly population-linked consumption.
2. **Productive inputs** — materials, fuel, components, and other goods required by local production capabilities.
3. **Operational / infrastructure demand** — repair goods, aircraft components, navigation equipment, maintenance inputs, and similar needs created by existing capital and transport activity.
4. **Exceptional demand** — temporary requirements created by shocks, damage, relief, reconstruction, special projects, or other events.
5. **Discretionary / luxury demand** — optional higher-order demand influenced more strongly by prosperity, culture, connectivity, and settlement function.

Population is therefore important but is not the universal driver of all demand. A small industrial or route-critical settlement may demand more machinery or aircraft parts than a larger agricultural settlement.

## Desired-stock model

For ordinary and intermediate goods, settlements should maintain bounded target inventory or desired-stock ranges rather than an abstract infinite demand curve.

Conceptually:

```text
current stock
    + local production
    + arrivals
    - consumption
    - productive use
        |
        v
compare against desired stock band
        |
        +-- below band -> stronger procurement / buy pressure
        +-- inside band -> ordinary market behavior
        +-- above band -> weaker buying / possible export surplus
```

Desired-stock depth may vary by commodity according to criticality, isolation, route reliability, storage practicality, and expected consumption. This supplies coarse resilience without a separate household or inventory-optimization simulator.

## Prosperity and composition

Prosperity should alter the composition of demand rather than merely multiplying every commodity equally.

Lower-prosperity or frontier settlements concentrate demand in subsistence goods, basic materials, tools, repair goods, and other essentials. More prosperous and connected settlements can support greater demand for finished goods, specialized components, furnishings, commercial services, and luxuries.

## Industry and capital demand

Industrial demand is generated by the input requirements of productive capabilities, independently of household consumption.

Durable capital goods such as aircraft and major machinery should generally not be modeled as continuous per-capita consumption. They should arise from discrete conditions such as:

- replacement;
- loss;
- justified capacity expansion;
- new service requirements;
- special projects;
- infrastructure changes.

This can create specific aircraft, machinery, delivery, manufacture, or financing opportunities without pretending settlements consume fractions of durable assets every economic period.

## Substitution

Demand may accept broader commodity classes where substitution is semantically reasonable, while specific industrial processes may require narrower subtypes.

Example:

```text
FOOD
  grain products
  vegetables
  meat
  preserved food
```

A general food requirement may accept multiple subtypes. A process requiring brass should not accept arbitrary goods merely because they share a broad category.

## Temporary event demand

Events add temporary demand state rather than rewriting baseline specialization.

Examples:

```text
route or beacon damage
 -> structural materials
 -> machinery
 -> navigation components

severe storm
 -> emergency provisions
 -> repair materials
```

Once satisfied or expired, this demand is removed or decays according to the event model.

## Contract and market relationship

Routine economic content must remain downstream of actual state:

```text
settlement need
 -> market buy pressure / shortage
 -> autonomous logistics attempts supply
 -> unresolved or urgent need
 -> Guild procurement / freight / repair contract
```

Authored narrative contracts may override this when story requires, but routine contracts should not fabricate shortages independently of civilization state.

## Player-facing information

The player should normally see lossy, serviceable economic information rather than raw simulation internals. Examples include:

- strong demand;
- critical shortage;
- adequate stock;
- current quoted price;
- current store availability.

ComputerCraft and Guild terminals may expose more structured information, but only through the established Guild information layer with provenance, permissions, and freshness. They should not expose unrestricted internal settlement-state vectors.

## Computational constraint

Demand formation must remain a coarse settlement-level calculation. A settlement need only maintain a modest vector of commodity state such as:

```text
commodity
current_stock
desired_stock / target band
baseline_consumption
productive_requirement
temporary_demand
```

Updates occur on coarse economic events or periods rather than per entity or per tick.

## Locked principles

> Settlement demand is derived from authoritative civilization population and settlement state, plus productive inputs, infrastructure requirements, prosperity, and temporary events. Skyforge does not simulate household transactions or individual consumer behavior.

> Demand is expressed primarily through bounded desired-stock and consumption pressures. Population dominates ordinary subsistence demand, while industry, trade activity, infrastructure, and settlement function drive specialized goods independently of population size.

> Durable capital goods such as aircraft and major machinery are demanded through discrete replacement, expansion, loss, or project conditions rather than continuous per-capita consumption.

> Routine prices, freight, procurement, and contracts respond to demand state rather than independently inventing it.
