# CIV-0003: Guild regionalization and structural grammar

**Status:** Precommitted authorship strategy
**Stage:** Pre-civilization; implementation deferred
**Date:** 2026-09-10

## Purpose

This record locks the current architectural direction for how Skyfarer's Guild buildings remain institutionally recognizable while adapting to biome, climate, local materials, and local construction traditions.

## 1. Regionalization principle

Guild regionalization follows the same underlying logic as biome-specific vanilla Minecraft villages, but at greater architectural depth.

The Guild preserves a stable institutional vocabulary and functional standards while allowing regional realization to change materially and climatically.

> Functional roles and institutional standards remain stable; regional materials, climate adaptations, roof forms, secondary ornament, and local construction traditions vary. No region should merely be a palette swap of another.

The intended pipeline is:

**Guild archetype**
→ **regional grammar**
→ **local settlement variation**
→ **history / wealth / modification state**

This should allow two Guild halls in different regions to be clearly related without appearing prefabricated, and two halls in the same region to remain distinct.

## 2. Guild invariants across regions

The following should remain recognizably Guild-owned or Guild-certified across most regions:

- canonical Open Sky crest;
- deep navy and aged brass as institutional accent colors where color is practical;
- deliberate, clearly signed public entrances;
- a compact family of recognizable window proportions;
- disciplined structural bay rhythm;
- standardized brass / iron service hardware;
- recognizable Guild lantern and bracket families;
- standardized plaques, hanging signs, route marks, and service markings;
- recurring public-service interior vocabulary: counters, registry fittings, contract boards, service windows;
- standardized freight doors, lifting points, moorings, and certified docking interfaces;
- signal, beacon, registry, and inspection hardware where function requires it.

Recognition must not depend on color alone. The architecture should remain legible as Guild in weathered, monochrome, or low-visibility conditions.

## 3. Regional realization examples

### 3.1 Temperate / plains reference grammar

Use this as the first standardization test because the strongest current donor assets map naturally to it.

Likely characteristics:

- stone / andesite / masonry foundation;
- dark expressed timber or metal structural frame;
- pale wall infill or plaster-like surfaces;
- slate / deepslate-like roof family;
- brass hardware;
- navy signs and banners;
- clear loading and public-entry hierarchy.

This is a reference grammar, not the universal material palette.

### 3.2 Northern / taiga

- steeper roofs and deeper eaves;
- heavier timber;
- stone-heavy lower stories;
- enclosed or protected loading areas;
- covered walkways;
- fewer delicate exposed fittings;
- dark iron used more heavily alongside brass;
- snow and wind management visibly integrated into design.

### 3.3 Mountain / alpine

- compact, anchored massing;
- masonry-heavy construction;
- retaining walls, buttressing, or partial rock integration;
- sheltered landing platforms;
- prominent signal / weather / navigation towers;
- limited large exposed openings;
- strong wind-conscious roof and circulation forms.

### 3.4 Dryland / desert

- thick pale masonry;
- shaded or smaller openings;
- internal courtyards and arcades where appropriate;
- flatter roofs where climate and local tradition justify them;
- cloth or canvas shade structures;
- bronze / terracotta / rust secondary palette;
- navy used as a focused institutional accent rather than a dominant wall color.

### 3.5 Coastal / humid

- raised foundations where flooding or spray requires it;
- weathered timber and stone quays;
- oxidized copper and marine hardware;
- covered galleries and loading porches;
- strong drainage treatment;
- shutters or storm-ready closures;
- visible crane, winch, rope, and mooring infrastructure.

Coastal Guild architecture may retain stronger cultural ancestry from maritime port practice.

### 3.6 Forest / woodland

- timber-forward construction;
- narrower or terrain-fitted footprints;
- covered exterior circulation;
- selective elevated walks;
- local woodworking traditions in secondary trim;
- standardized Guild doors, signs, counters, hardware, and crest treatments remain visible.

### 3.7 Agricultural / river valley

- granaries, weigh houses, procurement offices, broad warehouses, covered market yards;
- practical freight and crop-handling circulation;
- warm timber and local masonry;
- low ornament relative to service capacity;
- Guild identity expressed through dependable infrastructure rather than monumentality.

### 3.8 Industrial settlement

- brick / masonry / iron framing;
- large repetitive window bays;
- shallow or sawtooth industrial roofs where appropriate;
- cranes, gantries, service corridors, pipe runs, and repair bays;
- operational markings and weathering;
- navy and brass concentrated in offices, signs, certified machinery, and formal service zones rather than coating the entire facility.

## 4. Structural silhouette direction

The common Guild silhouette language is provisionally locked as:

> **strong masonry base + expressed structural frame + prominent roofline + disciplined repeated bays + unmistakably engineered additions**

This is not a fixed style sheet. It is the shared structural logic beneath regional variation.

### 4.1 Strong base

Guild buildings should tend to look grounded, durable, and built for long service life. Masonry, heavy timber sills, plinths, retaining elements, quays, or equivalent local foundations should visibly carry load and resist wear.

### 4.2 Expressed structure

The building should communicate how it stands up. Primary posts, beams, arches, trusses, braces, or metal frames should often be visible rather than hidden behind decorative surfaces.

This supports the Guild's identity as an institution of practical engineers, pilots, merchants, salvagers, surveyors, and mechanics.

### 4.3 Prominent roofline

Roofs should contribute meaningfully to silhouette and climate response.

- civic / commercial Guild buildings may favor moderate-to-steep pitches;
- industrial spans may use shallower or sawtooth forms;
- regional climate can strongly alter pitch, overhang, dormer, vent, and drainage treatment.

### 4.4 Disciplined bay rhythm

Windows, doors, loading bays, and structural supports should generally align to rational repeated spacing. This repeated rhythm is a major source of Guild cohesion across unrelated donor structures.

Exact dimensions remain deferred until in-game donor standardization tests.

### 4.5 Engineered additions

Guild buildings should often reveal adaptation over time:

- newer signal mast on an older hall;
- reinforced freight opening;
- added loading canopy;
- external crane or gantry;
- later repair shed;
- certified mooring hardware;
- new registry or communications wing.

Historical accretion is desirable. The Guild should feel old because it has continuously upgraded useful buildings, not because every structure is ancient and untouched.

## 5. Donor-standardization test

The first architectural validation should normalize several morphologically different donors into the temperate reference grammar while preserving their useful massing:

- Warehouse Row;
- Koin Train Station;
- Koin Victorian Factory / Factory Yard;
- one manor, stable, or frontier-scale donor.

The human-eye question is:

> Do these unrelated structures read as facilities of the same institution after standardization, while still retaining useful differences of function and scale?

Only after this test should exact block palettes, bay dimensions, roof-module dimensions, window modules, and freight clearances be frozen.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

The regionalization and structural grammar succeeds if:

1. Guild ownership is recognizable before reading signage;
2. regional versions look climatically and materially plausible;
3. no region looks like a simple recolor of the temperate reference;
4. the Open Sky emblem remains unmistakably canonical everywhere;
5. functional interfaces remain standardized even when architectural shells vary;
6. unrelated donor buildings can be normalized without erasing their strongest authored morphology;
7. the result reads as one long-lived institution embedded in many local cultures.
