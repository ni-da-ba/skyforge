# CIV-0010: Guild membership and delegated authority

**Status:** Precommitted design direction
**Stage:** Pre-civilization; implementation deferred
**Date:** 2026-09-10

## Decision

Guild progression should be **authorization-based rather than rank-based**.

Membership grants ordinary participation in the Guild network. Higher Standing should unlock specific authorizations, responsibilities, and scopes of delegated trust rather than abstract RPG ranks.

The Guild should care primarily about **what the player is authorized to do**, not what nominal level they have reached.

## Participation states

The following broad states are useful as conceptual categories, but should not become a rigid level ladder:

- **public / non-member** — may use basic public-facing Guild services where available, such as information, ordinary trade, and some transport services;
- **Guild member** — participates normally in the Guild network and may access contracts, accounts, registration, insurance eligibility, licenses, and member services;
- **authorized operator** — a trusted independent professional eligible for sensitive work, bonded cargo, recovery activity, Guild-property operations, or other delegated responsibilities;
- **Guild agent / factor** — may act on behalf of the Guild within an explicitly defined operational scope and may operate approved Guild-facing infrastructure.

These are statuses and authorizations, not numerical ranks.

## Membership

Membership should be attainable relatively early. It is a gateway into full participation, not an endgame achievement.

The Bellanca opening may plausibly establish or accelerate the player's membership and account relationship as part of the initial claim, restitution, registration, and licensing flow.

Membership may require Scrip for dues, fees, licenses, or bonds, but payment alone does not create Standing.

Canonical distinction:

> Paying for membership grants participation. It does not purchase institutional trust.

## Standing and authorization

Standing determines what the Guild is willing to entrust to the player.

Examples of discrete authorizations may include:

- bonded freight authorization;
- recovery authorization;
- Guild property custody;
- sensitive or dangerous contract eligibility;
- independent inspection authority;
- advanced certification authority;
- emergency-response authority;
- restricted technical-record access;
- agency authorization;
- discretionary / non-public commission eligibility.

Different careers may accumulate different authorization portfolios. A trusted freight operator need not also be a trusted salvage specialist.

The system should therefore avoid a universal ladder in which every player progresses through the same sequence of Guild titles.

## Delegated scope

Delegated authority must remain **scoped**.

Examples:

- authority to recover a named registered vessel is not authority to seize arbitrary registered vessels;
- emergency custody of Guild property is not permanent command authority;
- authorization to inspect a specific class of craft does not imply universal certification power;
- authority to operate a local Guild agency does not confer sovereignty or general policing powers.

This preserves the Guild's non-sovereign character and creates room for meaningful jurisdictional disputes when individual chapters exceed their authority.

## Player-run Guild facilities

High Standing may unlock one of the strongest long-term institutional rewards: permission to operate **Guild-recognized infrastructure at a player-built base**.

This should not be granted through a single craftable Guild Hall block. Instead, the Guild authorizes the player to establish an agency or recognized operating post after relevant requirements are satisfied.

Candidate requirements may include:

- recognized or registered property / base;
- suitable landing or docking access;
- Open Sky identification and required Guild signage;
- a service counter or administrative point;
- capability-specific equipment;
- compliance with any required standardized interfaces;
- inspection or validation.

Once approved, the site may participate in the wider Guild network according to its authorized capabilities.

Possible effects include:

- Guild contracts appearing there;
- bonded freight being accepted or dispatched;
- recognized cargo handoff;
- route or rescue infrastructure using the site;
- Guild aircraft or agents occasionally visiting;
- service fees or other compensation for useful network activity.

The player should build and operate the physical site. The Guild supplies legitimacy, standards, and network integration rather than constructing the base for them.

## Optional progression

Operating Guild infrastructure should remain elective. A player may instead remain an independent skyfarer, manufacturer, trader, explorer, or mobile airship operator without becoming a Guild administrator.

The reward is institutional integration, not mandatory management gameplay.

## Independent factor model

At high Standing, the player should feel less like a salaried Guild employee and more like a trusted independent factor, contractor, or field agent.

This supports sensitive or discretionary commissions that cannot plausibly appear on the public contract board.

The Guild may therefore trust the player to:

- protect Guild property;
- investigate losses or wrongdoing;
- resolve exceptional disputes;
- perform politically sensitive recovery or logistical work;
- act under limited delegated authority during emergencies.

These opportunities should be unlocked by demonstrated trust rather than purchased rank.

## Precommitted rule

> **Guild progression is authorization-based rather than rank-based. Membership grants ordinary participation; Standing enables increasingly sensitive and consequential authorizations. At high Standing, the player may be trusted to operate Guild-recognized infrastructure or act as an independent Guild agent within explicitly delegated scopes.**

A major long-term sandbox payoff should be:

> **Civilization begins trusting infrastructure the player built.**

Exact authorization names, thresholds, inspection procedures, dues, and agency-validation rules remain implementation-stage decisions.
