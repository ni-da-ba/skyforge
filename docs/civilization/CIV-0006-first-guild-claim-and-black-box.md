# CIV-0006: First Guild claim and black-box reversal

**Status:** Precommitted narrative / systems direction
**Stage:** Pre-civilization; implementation deferred
**Date:** 2026-09-10

## Decision

The player's first substantive interaction with the Skyfarer's Guild should be a claim dispute tied to the crashed Bellanca B0-A.

The Bellanca carries a recoverable flight recorder / black-box artifact. The Guild initially evaluates the crash under an unfavorable interpretation for the player and offers a limited or otherwise tough settlement. The player can then present or recover the recorder evidence. Information contained in the recorder establishes that the Guild, a Guild-certified service, or another Guild-controlled responsibility materially contributed to the loss. The Guild's position changes, and the player is offered meaningful restitution options.

The scene should teach the Guild through procedure, evidence, liability, and restitution rather than exposition.

## Tone

The Guild should be stern, procedural, and protective of its obligations, but not cartoonishly predatory.

The initial unfavorable position should be plausible under the information then available. The reversal should occur because new evidence changes the claim, not because the clerk suddenly becomes generous.

This is important to the Guild's long-term identity: the institution may negotiate hard and rely on rules, but its legitimacy depends on honoring records, contracts, and proven obligations.

## Recommended opening flow

1. **Crash** — the player begins beside the wrecked Bellanca.
2. **Recovery** — among the recoverable items is a flight recorder / black box, or enough evidence to locate one in the wreck.
3. **Guild contact** — the player reaches the local Guild Hall and presents registration / ownership evidence.
4. **Initial claim determination** — absent recorder evidence, the Guild treats the loss as primarily the player's responsibility or as outside full coverage and proposes a weak settlement.
5. **Evidence review** — recorder data reveals a Guild-side cause or liability-bearing failure.
6. **Claim reversal** — the Guild acknowledges responsibility under its own standards and materially improves the settlement.
7. **Restitution choice** — the player chooses among several viable remedies, such as replacement aircraft, Scrip compensation, or retaining the wreck with a reduced cash settlement and support for repair.
8. **Ongoing relationship** — the claim establishes the player's account, registry relationship, basic standing, and access to ordinary Guild services and contracts.

## Liability source guidance

The exact cause should be selected later, but it should satisfy three constraints:

- the failure is understandable from recorder evidence;
- responsibility traces to something the Guild plausibly certifies, maintains, dispatches, insures, or operates;
- the cause introduces a useful Skyforge system without requiring dense legal exposition.

Plausible categories include:

- failure of a Guild-certified component or maintenance action;
- incorrect or stale Guild navigation / hazard information;
- a faulty Guild beacon, routing instruction, or dispatch decision;
- a documented defect that should have been caught by Guild inspection;
- another covered operational failure clearly inside Guild responsibility.

Avoid causes that require the player to understand a complicated insurance contract before the scene works.

## Restitution options

The preferred choice set remains:

- **replacement aircraft** — quickest route back into normal flight;
- **Scrip settlement** — maximum economic flexibility;
- **retain wreck + reduced settlement** — supports engineering, repair, salvage, and player ownership of the original Bellanca.

The third option should be genuinely viable rather than a trap.

## Black-box function beyond the opening

The flight recorder should ideally introduce a reusable world concept rather than exist only as a tutorial key item.

Potential later uses include:

- reconstructing aircraft losses;
- validating insurance claims;
- salvage identification;
- diagnosing component failures;
- recovering route / flight metadata;
- establishing fault in exceptional contracts or disputes.

The opening therefore acts as the first demonstration of a broader evidence-and-records system.

## UX constraint

Do not turn the first claim into a long dialogue tree or legal mini-game.

The intended player experience is compact:

**bad offer → recover/present evidence → clear factual reversal → meaningful restitution choice**

The player should understand why the outcome changed without reading pages of policy text.

## Precommitted rule

> The player's first Guild relationship is established through a contested Bellanca claim in which recovered black-box evidence changes the facts, reveals Guild-side liability, and converts an initially unfavorable settlement into meaningful restitution.

Exact dialogue, liability mechanism, payout values, recorder schema, and aircraft-replacement details remain implementation-stage decisions.
