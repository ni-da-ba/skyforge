# CIV-0003: Open Sky regional and contextual variants

**Status:** Precommitted visual-identity guidance
**Stage:** Pre-civilization; final asset implementation deferred
**Date:** 2026-09-10

## Purpose

This record preserves the decision that the Skyfarer's Guild uses one canonical emblem — **Open Sky** — while allowing regional and contextual treatments that remain visibly subordinate to the same mark.

## Canonical invariant

The Open Sky emblem remains the same institutional mark everywhere. Regional identity may alter mounting, material realization, secondary symbols, border treatment, wear, and supporting color, but must not replace the core star + open sky field + upward wing/cradle geometry.

Core Guild colors remain **deep navy + aged brass / muted gold** where color is available.

## Regional treatments

### Northern / alpine chapter
Context: cold, mountainous, high-wind settlements; navigation through passes, snow, and cloud.
- Crest material: dark iron, pale stone, brass inlay.
- Secondary mark: mountain silhouette or three peaks beneath the crest.
- Supporting colors: slate, cold gray, off-white.
- Architectural use: carved stone plaques, heavy vertical banners, compact signal beacons.
- Visual character: austere, durable, weather-resistant.

### Coastal / maritime-influenced chapter
Context: settlements with strong dock, fishing, storm, and maritime traditions adapted to sky travel.
- Crest material: oxidized copper, dark timber, brass fittings.
- Secondary mark: wave, spiral current, or stylized cloud-sea line beneath the crest.
- Supporting colors: sea green, weathered teal, cream.
- Architectural use: hanging signs, mast flags, warehouse plaques, cargo seals.
- Visual character: mercantile, weathered, navigational.

### Frontier chapter
Context: sparse or newly linked settlements where Guild services are practical and limited.
- Crest material: painted wood, stamped iron, simple brass plate.
- Secondary mark: pine, ridge, trail marker, or local landmark.
- Supporting colors: umber, olive, canvas, soot black.
- Architectural use: simple wall signs, single exterior banner, branded crates and registry plates.
- Visual character: utilitarian, repaired, locally fabricated.

### Dryland / desert chapter
Context: arid, exposed settlements where shade, heat, dust, and long-distance logistics dominate.
- Crest material: pale stone, dark bronze, brass.
- Secondary mark: mesa, sun line, dryland horizon, or wind motif.
- Supporting colors: sandstone, muted rust, cream.
- Architectural use: recessed plaques, shaded hanging signs, cloth standards with reinforced edges.
- Visual character: sun-faded but formally maintained.

### Industrial chapter
Context: factory districts, heavy repair, foundries, large freight terminals, and major workshops.
- Crest material: blackened steel, brass, riveted plate, enamel.
- Secondary mark: gear, hammerless tool motif, turbine, or abstract machinery mark.
- Supporting colors: charcoal, oxide brown, steel gray.
- Architectural use: machinery castings, gantry signs, inspection plates, loading-bay stencils.
- Visual character: engineered, robust, serviceable.

### Agricultural / provisioning chapter
Context: grain, livestock, food processing, rural market hubs, provisioning routes.
- Crest material: painted timber, local stone, brass plaques.
- Secondary mark: grain sheaf, leaf, orchard branch, or granary form.
- Supporting colors: muted green, wheat, warm brown.
- Architectural use: storehouses, procurement depots, market halls, contract boards.
- Visual character: civic, prosperous, practical.

### Scholastic / navigational chapter
Context: Guild academy, charting office, observatory, survey institute, meteorology station.
- Crest material: polished stone, brass, dark enamel.
- Secondary mark: astrolabe line, chart grid, telescope, or small secondary star.
- Supporting colors: ink blue, cream, brass, restrained white.
- Architectural use: formal seals, library floors, map rooms, observatory entrances.
- Visual character: precise, scholarly, old-institutional.

### Salvage / recovery chapter
Context: rescue stations, wreck recovery, insurance claims, Sky Dredger operations.
- Crest material: dark steel, brass, high-visibility painted backing where needed.
- Secondary mark: hook, lifeline loop, buoy-like ring, or downward recovery line.
- Supporting colors: navy, brass, weathered orange as an operational accent only.
- Architectural use: rescue beacons, recovery craft, salvage yard markings, claim offices.
- Visual character: unmistakably Guild but more operational and safety-oriented.

## Contextual render variants

### Ceremonial
Full emblem with refined brass edging, used at chapter halls, charters, major exchanges, formal interiors, and memorials.

### Standard civic
Simplified full emblem for façades, hanging signs, uniforms, aircraft, and public-facing service buildings.

### Utility / cargo
Reduced emblem for crates, cargo seals, inspection marks, machinery castings, and registry plates. Must remain traceable to the full Open Sky mark.

### Signal / navigation
Emphasizes the central star and circular sky field while retaining the lower wing/cradle silhouette. Used on signal towers, beacons, route markers, and navigation equipment.

### Monochrome / carved
Single-material geometry for stone, iron, stamps, paper seals, and low-color environments.

## Design rule

Regional variation should answer **"where is this Guild chapter?"** while the invariant Open Sky mark answers **"who operates this place?"**.

If a regional treatment becomes recognizable without the Open Sky core, it has become too independent and should be reduced.

## Deferred validation

Final art implementation should compare all major regional treatments at:
- façade scale;
- hanging-sign scale;
- banner scale;
- aircraft-tail scale;
- crate/stamp scale;
- monochrome carving;
- Minecraft pixel/banner approximation.
